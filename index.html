Discovery Phase
Problem Statement
Construction is one of the world's most dangerous occupations. We mapped the root causes, pain points, and opportunities through a rigorous Design Thinking process.
01

🧍 User / Protagonist

Bimal Khan — A 30-year-old daily wage construction worker who works at heights, operates heavy machinery, and is exposed to dust, noise, and electrical hazards daily.

02

⚠️ Pain Points & Needs

Lack of proper PPE · Poor safety training · Long hours in risky conditions · Unsafe scaffolding · No medical support during accidents · Dependence on daily wage income.

03

🔍 Root Causes (5 Whys)

Safety rules not strictly followed · Contractors cut safety costs · Workers receive limited training · Poor monitoring and supervision · No advanced safety technology deployed on site.

04

📊 Evidence Base

~165 out of every 1,000 construction workers are injured during work activities. Major causes: falls, electrocution, lack of PPE, machinery hazards. Many Indian sites still lack helmets, gloves, and boots.

Point of View Statement

Construction site workers like Bimal Khan need safer and easier ways to work in hazardous construction environments because lack of proper safety systems, training, and protective equipment increases the risk of injuries, stress, and accidents during daily work activities.

How Might We…?
HMW 1

How might we help construction workers stay safe while working in risky environments like heights, machinery zones, and electrical areas?

HMW 2

How might we reduce workplace accidents and injuries so that construction workers can work confidently and safely?

HMW 3

How might we make it easier for construction workers to use safety equipment and follow safety procedures during daily tasks?

Key Insight

Workers are aware of risks but continue in unsafe environments due to financial pressure. They want to follow safety practices but face challenges: lack of PPE, limited training, and dependence on supervisors.

Contradiction

Workers say they understand the importance of safety equipment, but they sometimes work without helmets or gloves when equipment is uncomfortable or unavailable — driven by pressure to complete tasks.

Primary Research
Field Observations & Interviews
We conducted field visits to two types of construction sites in Hyderabad, observing working conditions and interviewing workers and supervisors directly.
Site 01

Corporate Construction

Workers provided with high-quality safety equipment including helmets and PPE.

Organised, systematic environment with clear supervision.

Fewer working hours compared to smaller sites.

Daily wage approximately ₹400/day with accommodation provided.

Safety measures and inspection clearly visible on site.

⭐ Safety & worker welfare prioritised
Site 02

Independent Construction

Workers do not use proper safety equipment — no helmets or protective gear observed.

Less organised, lacks supervision. No safety officer present.

Workers work ~12 hours per day in hazardous conditions.

Daily wage approximately ₹1,200/day but no facilities or accommodation.

No emergency support, first aid, or medical coverage available.

⚠️ Higher wages; safety completely neglected

Secondary Research
Global Context & Laws
We studied worker safety laws in India and the US, Telangana Government Orders, foreign construction technology, and wage disparities to contextualise our solution.
🇮🇳

Key Indian Laws

Building & Construction Workers Act · Factories Act · Minimum Wages Act · Payment of Wages Act · Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions Code 2020 — all mandate safety, yet enforcement remains inconsistent.

🇺🇸

US Safety Standards

OSHA mandates mandatory PPE (no PPE = removed from site), daily toolbox talks, fall protection systems above 6 feet, and frequent inspections with heavy fines. Workers are certified before operating machinery.

📋

Telangana GOs

G.O.Ms.No. 4 (2020): Employers must provide helmets, gloves, goggles free of cost. G.O.Rt.No. 23 (2022): OSH Code implemented state-wide. Digital inspection and online labour compliance systems mandated.

🤖

Technology Gap

US uses GPS-guided excavators, AI-powered cranes, and automated safety monitoring. India still relies on manual supervision and semi-automatic machines. Advanced tech adoption is increasing but slowly.

💰

Wage Disparity

US construction wages are high due to cost of living, FLSA enforcement, health insurance mandates, and skilled-labour demand. Indian wages are lower due to surplus labour supply and fewer enforced worker benefits.

Prototype Phase
Prototype Mechanics
The WorkShield prototype is a bench-assembled quadcopter with an onboard AI compute module, camera gimbal, and sensor suite — designed for iterative testing and deployment.
· PPE Violation Detection — Computer Vision Pipeline

🚁 Mechanical Assembly

The F450 frame holds four 2212 brushless motors at each arm. The Pixhawk flight controller sits centrally for vibration isolation. A 2-axis gimbal stabilises the Pi Camera to ensure clear video even during turns. All electronics are mounted on a custom 3D-printed ABS tray inside the frame.

  • Carbon fibre arms + central ABS hub
  • Vibration-dampened FC mount
  • Custom 3D-printed sensor pod underneath
  • Balanced propellers: 2× CW + 2× CCW

🧠 Software Stack

The Raspberry Pi 4 runs a Python inference loop at 5fps using YOLOv8-nano. Detected violations trigger audio via PyAudio and push a JSON alert over 4G to a Node.js cloud backend. A simple web dashboard allows supervisors to view the live stream and incident log.

  • YOLOv8-nano — ultralytics Python library
  • OpenCV — frame capture + annotation
  • Flask API — local REST endpoint
  • Node.js + MQTT — cloud alert broker

📡 Patrol Logic

The Pixhawk autopilot runs ArduCopter firmware. A pre-programmed GPS waypoint mission is uploaded via Mission Planner. The drone loops around the construction perimeter at 15m altitude, hovering at inspection points for 10s before moving to the next.

  • ArduCopter autonomous waypoint mode
  • Return-to-home on low battery (<20%)
  • Obstacle avoidance: HC-SR04 front sensor
  • Telemetry link to ground station PC

⚡ Power Management

A 3S 5200mAh LiPo provides ~20 minutes of flight time under full load. A power distribution board (PDB) routes 12V to motors via ESCs and steps down to 5V for the Raspberry Pi and sensors. A voltage alarm triggers RTH at 10.5V (20% capacity).

  • PDB with integrated BEC 5V / 3A
  • LiPo voltage monitor + buzzer alarm
  • Total draw: ~18A hover, ~28A peak
  • Charging: B6AC balance charger, 1C rate
Development Phase
Weekly Roadmap
A 10-week structured project timeline — from problem discovery to final prototype validation and documentation.
Week 1–2
Problem Discovery & Field Research
Conducted field visits to two construction sites in Hyderabad. Interviewed workers and supervisors. Documented observations, collected audio and video evidence. Created Problem Statement Canvas and Empathy Map.
Week 3–4
Secondary Research & User Journey Mapping
Studied Indian and US worker safety laws, Telangana Government Orders, global construction technology, and wage factors. Created user journey map for Bimal Khan. Validated proto-persona assumptions.
Week 5–6
Ideation — 6-3-5 Brainwriting (72 Ideas)
All 8 team members participated in structured 6-3-5 brainwriting session generating 72 ideas. Ran dot-voting and feasibility matrix to shortlist top ideas. Selected autonomous drone as primary solution.
Week 7–8
Hardware Procurement & Assembly
Sourced all components: F450 frame, Raspberry Pi 4, Pixhawk FC, motors, ESCs, LiPo battery, camera, GPS, and sensors. Assembled and calibrated the quadcopter. Conducted bench tests for ESC calibration and motor spin-up.
Week 9
AI Model Integration & Software Development
Trained YOLOv8-nano model on a dataset of construction site images. Deployed inference pipeline on Raspberry Pi. Built Flask API for alert routing. Integrated 4G upload and supervisor dashboard. Conducted indoor simulation tests.
Week 10
Testing, Validation & Final Documentation
Conducted outdoor flight tests and PPE detection accuracy trials. Measured system latency and alert responsiveness. Compiled all documentation — field reports, design thinking artifacts, technical specs, and simulation results.

Future Scope
What's Next
WorkShield is a foundation. The roadmap beyond the current prototype expands detection, autonomy, and integration across all construction sites.
🔋

Charging Dock & Continuous Patrol

An auto-landing charging dock allows the drone to return, recharge, and resume patrol — enabling 24/7 monitoring without manual intervention.

🤖

Multi-Drone Swarm

Deploy a coordinated swarm of 3–5 drones across large construction sites with a central coordination server dividing the site into coverage zones.

🌡️

Thermal & Gas Sensing

Add a FLIR Lepton thermal camera and advanced gas sensor array to detect heat stress, fire risk, and chemical exposure across the site in real time.

📱

Worker Safety App

A bilingual app (Hindi + Telugu) giving workers access to their personal safety score, incident history, PPE compliance record, and emergency SOS button.

🏛️

Government Integration

Link violation logs directly to Telangana Labour Department portals under G.O.Ms.No. 4 (2020), enabling automated compliance reporting and penalty enforcement.

🧠

Predictive Risk AI

Train a time-series model on historical violation data to predict high-risk zones and time windows — enabling proactive safety interventions before accidents occur.

Conclusion
Mission Accomplished
"WorkShield demonstrates that construction worker safety can be actively monitored, not just regulated — bridging the gap between policy and practice through autonomous AI."

WorkShield emerged from real conversations with real workers — people like Bimal Khan, who risk their lives daily for wages as low as ₹400. Through rigorous Design Thinking — field observation, empathy mapping, 72 ideas, and hands-on prototyping — we built a drone-based system capable of detecting PPE violations, alerting workers in real time, and logging incidents for supervisors and regulatory bodies.

The system achieves 82% PPE detection accuracy with under 300ms alert latency, operating for 20 minutes per flight at a hardware cost of ₹43,300. While construction remains India's most dangerous occupation, technology like WorkShield offers a scalable, affordable, and immediately deployable path to making sites safer — not through adding paperwork, but by adding eyes in the sky that never blink.
